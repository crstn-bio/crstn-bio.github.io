## Background

Low-intensity transcranial focused ultrasound (TUS) can modulate motor-cortex excitability without surgery. In Parkinson’s disease, accelerated theta-burst TUS (a-tbTUS), three sonications 30 minutes apart, increased corticospinal excitability measured as TMS-evoked MEP amplitude. In healthy people, a single theta-burst session followed by functional electrical stimulation (FES) produced transient inhibition, which suggests the central and peripheral stimulation interact (homeostatic metaplasticity). Whether multisession a-tbTUS combined with FES helps people with Parkinson’s is unknown.

## Design

- **Groups.** Participants are randomized to real or sham a-tbTUS, both followed by FES.
- **Intervention.** At each of four visits: a-tbTUS to both primary motor cortices (three sonications, 30 minutes apart), then 45 minutes of FES to arm and hand muscles on both sides.
- **Outcomes.** At visits 1 and 4: resting motor threshold and average peak-to-peak MEP amplitude at the intensity that gives about 1 mV (TMS), MDS-UPDRS Part III, and a finger-tapping task.

## Hypothesis

Real a-tbTUS with FES will act synergistically: a larger increase in MEP amplitude and a greater improvement in MDS-UPDRS Part III than sham a-tbTUS with FES.
